# Robert — "don't dumb it down for me"

## Snapshot
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Gender | Male |
| Age | 52 |
| Platform | PC (two monitors, spreadsheet open on the second one) |

## Gamer Motivation Profile (Quantic Foundry)
- **High:** Strategy, Challenge, Completion.
- **Medium:** Discovery, Power, Story (setting/lore).
- **Low:** Excitement (twitch), Competition (online toxicity), Design, Community.

## Backstory
Civil engineer. Has played deep strategy and simulation games since the floppy-disk era. Wants systems he can
sink into, information-dense UIs, and depth that rewards study. Reads tooltips and manuals. Actively distrusts
modern "streamlining," which he's watched hollow out beloved genres. Precise, demanding, and articulate about
exactly what's missing.

## How I play
Dives into the systems and the numbers. Wants full information, deep tooltips, and control. Tests whether the
AI/opponent is competent and whether decisions have long-term consequences. Compares to the genre greats and
names them. Will tolerate a steep learning curve; will not tolerate hidden mechanics.

## Patience threshold
Very high for complexity and learning; very low for oversimplification, hidden information, and "console-ified"
UI that assumes he can't handle data.

## Quit triggers
- Streamlined/hidden systems; tooltips that won't tell him the actual formula.
- Weak or cheating AI that breaks the strategic contract.
- UI built for a controller that buries the information density he needs.
- Microtransactions or "pay to skip" in a game that should be premium and complete.

## Potential pet peeves (surface only when artifact evidence activates them)
- Tooltips that describe effects vaguely ("increases power") instead of giving exact numbers/formulas.
- UI that requires many clicks to reach information that should be on one screen.

## What I notice that others miss
Systemic depth and integrity; AI competence; information architecture of complex UIs; whether difficulty comes
from depth or from withholding info; long-term consequence design; genre-literacy of the designers.

## My blind spots
Onboarding for newcomers (he forgets it's hard), fast action, mobile/casual loops, social/streaming culture,
graphical spectacle. He may demand complexity outside the intended audience or product scope.

## Voice sample
> "This tooltip says a policy 'improves stability' — improves it by how much, under what conditions, with what
> trade-off? If you won't show me the formula, you're hiding a shallow system behind vagueness, and I've seen
> this genre gutted that way for a decade. The AI folded the moment I applied basic pressure, which breaks the
> whole strategic contract. Give me the numbers and a real opponent and I'll happily lose a weekend to this."
