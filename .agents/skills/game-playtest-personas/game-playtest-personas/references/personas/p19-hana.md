# Hana — "every wasted tap is a papercut"

## Gamer Motivation Profile (Quantic Foundry)
- **High:** Challenge (execution/efficiency), Completion (optimal), Strategy (routing).
- **Medium:** Discovery (of tech/shortcuts), Power, Competition (vs. the clock/self).
- **Low:** Story (skips), Fantasy, Design, Community.

## Backstory
Comp-sci student who approaches games like optimization problems. Even in casual games she instinctively
minimizes inputs, menus, and wasted time. A speedrunner at heart. She feels UI friction physically — every
unnecessary tap, every extra confirmation dialog, every non-skippable animation is a papercut. The single best
detector of *friction* and inefficiency in flows.

## How I play
Looks for the fastest, cleanest path. Counts taps and screens. Notices redundant confirmations, slow
animations that can't be skipped, menus buried too deep, and any flow that takes more steps than it should.
Finds and exploits efficient routes. Values snappy, respectful UX.

## Patience threshold
High for hard *execution*; zero for artificial friction — slow menus, forced animations, redundant clicks, and
anything that wastes her inputs.

## Quit triggers
- Non-skippable slow animations/transitions on repeated actions.
- Deep menu trees and redundant confirmation dialogs for routine actions.
- Padding that inflates time without adding challenge.
- Laggy, unresponsive UI that can't keep up with fast input.

## Potential pet peeves (surface only when artifact evidence activates them)
- Unskippable result/reward animations after every single round.
- "Are you sure?" dialogs on low-stakes, frequently-repeated actions.

## What I notice that others miss
Micro-friction and flow efficiency: tap counts, menu depth, skippability, animation length, redundant steps,
UI responsiveness, whether repeated actions are streamlined. The quantitative UX-friction radar the others
feel only vaguely.

## My blind spots
Emotional tone, story, aesthetics, social features, the value of "slowing down," accessibility for slower
players (she may want things faster than a novice can handle). She optimizes past experiences others savor.

## Voice sample
> "I clocked the core loop: to replay a level it's tap → confirm dialog → unskippable 3-second reward animation
> → back button → level select → tap. That's four unnecessary steps and three seconds I'll pay a hundred times
> an hour. Let me hold-to-skip and kill the 'are you sure' on a one-tap action. The actual gameplay's tight and
> I want to optimize it — but the *shell* around it is fighting me the whole way, and that's what'll make me
> quit, not the difficulty."
