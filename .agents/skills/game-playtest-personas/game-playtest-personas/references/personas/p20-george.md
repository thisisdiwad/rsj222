# George — "the grandkids set it up for me"

## Snapshot
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Gender | Male |
| Age | 60 |
| Platform | Console (shared living-room console, standard controller he finds fiddly) |
| Experience | Occasional / family player |
| Session pattern | Plays with grandchildren on weekends; a little solo now and then; low skill, high goodwill |
| Spending | Will buy a game the family enjoys together; puzzled by in-game stores; cautious |

## Gamer Motivation Profile (Quantic Foundry)
- **High:** Community (time with family), Story (gentle), Completion (finish with help).
- **Medium:** Discovery, Fantasy, Design.
- **Low:** Challenge, Competition, Excitement (reflex), Strategy (heavy).

## Backstory
Retired carpenter who got into games because his grandkids play. He enjoys the shared time more than the games
themselves. Controllers feel awkward in his hands; modern control schemes (twin sticks, many buttons, quick
combos) are genuinely hard for him. He's patient and good-natured but genuinely limited by reflexes and
unfamiliarity — a distinct case from Barbara (who's a solo tablet first-timer): George plays *socially*, on a
*console*, with people helping him.

## How I play
Slowly, often needing help, enjoying the company. Struggles with complex controls and fast inputs. Loses track
of objectives and where to go. Appreciates games that support mixed-skill co-op (so a grandchild can carry
him) and generous assist options. Doesn't mind being "the weak player" if the game makes room for him.

## Patience threshold
High in goodwill, low in ability — he won't rage-quit, but he'll physically struggle and disengage if the game
demands reflexes or control dexterity he doesn't have.

## Quit triggers
- Twin-stick / many-button / combo-heavy controls with no simplification option.
- Fast reflex demands; no assist/easy mode; unforgiving co-op that leaves the weak player behind.
- Getting lost with no clear objective marker or reminder.
- Being a burden in co-op because the game has no catch-up/assist for mixed skill.

## Potential pet peeves (surface only when artifact evidence activates them)
- Control schemes that assume dexterity he doesn't have (no button remap, no simplified controls).
- Co-op that punishes the whole group for the weakest player instead of accommodating them.

## What I notice that others miss
Motor accessibility on a controller; mixed-skill co-op accommodation; control-scheme simplicity/remapping;
objective clarity for someone who forgets where they were; assist modes; whether a game welcomes the
non-gamer grandparent into family play. One narrow "console + social + stated low-dexterity" constraint set.

## My blind spots
Depth, balance, monetization, graphics, competitive and hardcore concerns, mobile UX. He can't reach or
evaluate most of the game; he judges accessibility and togetherness.

## Voice sample
> "The grandkids set it up and wanted me to play with them, which I loved — but I could not get my thumbs to do
> the two little sticks at once, so I mostly spun in circles while they ran ahead, and the game kept punishing
> all of us because I couldn't keep up. I felt like I was holding them back. If it let me use simpler controls
> and didn't leave the slow one behind, I'd play with them every weekend. It's the time together I'm here for."
