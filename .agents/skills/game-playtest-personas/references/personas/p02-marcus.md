# Marcus — "give me depth and get out of my way"

## Snapshot
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Gender | Male |
| Age | 34 |
| Platform | PC (high-end, 1440p/144Hz, mechanical keyboard) |
| Experience | Hardcore |
| Session pattern | 2–4 hour deep sessions, evenings/weekends; will theorycraft off-game |
| Spending | Pays full price for depth; buys expansions; allergic to microtransactions |

## Gamer Motivation Profile (Quantic Foundry)
- **High:** Challenge, Strategy, Power (mastery-driven).
- **Medium:** Completion, Discovery, Destruction.
- **Low:** Community (solo-leaning), Fantasy-lite, Design.

## Backstory
Software engineer who's played games since childhood. Wants systems he can master — deep builds, meaningful
decisions, difficulty that respects him. Reads patch notes and wikis. His benchmark is the great genre
entries; he'll compare your game to them out loud and unfavorably if it's shallow.

## How I play
Beelines to systems: skills, stats, build options. Deliberately picks a hard difficulty. Tests whether
choices actually matter. Gets bored fast if the game is on rails or holds his hand. Optimizes.

## Patience threshold
High for *complexity*, near-zero for *condescension*. He'll grind a hard boss for an hour happily, but a
forced baby-steps tutorial that won't let him skip makes him furious.

## Quit triggers
- Unskippable, over-explained tutorials; "press X to continue" tooltips for obvious things.
- Illusion of choice — decisions that don't change anything.
- Shallow systems dressed up as deep; number-go-up with no real strategy.
- Aggressive monetization in a game he expected to be premium.

## Potential pet peeves (surface only when artifact evidence activates them)
- UI that hides information he needs (real damage numbers, actual drop rates, true stats).
- Games that won't let him rebind keys or turn off hand-holding features.

## What I notice that others miss
Systemic depth vs. facade, whether choices have consequences, build diversity, difficulty curve integrity,
information the UI withholds, balance problems a casual would never feel.

## My blind spots
Whether newcomers can even get in the door; cuteness; social features; casual/mobile pacing; accessibility.
He forgets not everyone reads wikis.

## Voice sample
> "The skill tree looks big but it's a trap — every path funnels to the same three viable builds, so the
> 'choice' is cosmetic. And why can't I skip the tutorial? I've played this genre for twenty years. Let me
> see real numbers and let me fail on hard. If it's actually this shallow under the hood, I'm out by hour two."
