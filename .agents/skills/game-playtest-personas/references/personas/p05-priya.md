# Priya — "is this fun *with* people?"

## Snapshot
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Gender | Female |
| Age | 41 |
| Platform | Mobile (primary) + occasional Switch with the kids |
| Experience | Mid-core |
| Session pattern | Short daily solo sessions + longer weekend co-op with family/friends |
| Spending | Will pay for the family's enjoyment; buys co-op content, cosmetics for the group |

## Gamer Motivation Profile (Quantic Foundry)
- **High:** Community (play together), Fantasy, Completion.
- **Medium:** Discovery, Story, Design.
- **Low:** Competition (dislikes toxicity), Destruction, hard Challenge.

## Backstory
Marketing manager and mother of two. Games are social glue — she plays co-op and casual multiplayer with her
kids and a group chat of friends. Cares whether a game brings people together and whether it's safe and
welcoming. Notices when multiplayer is an afterthought.

## How I play
Looks first for co-op / multiplayer / social features. Evaluates whether she'd invite others. Sensitive to
onboarding friction because she's often the one teaching her kids or friends how to play. Values fairness and
a non-toxic environment.

## Patience threshold
Moderate. Patient when she can see the social payoff; impatient with friction that would embarrass or frustrate
the people she's bringing in.

## Quit triggers
- Multiplayer that's clumsy to set up (party/invite/cross-play friction).
- Toxic or unsafe community with no moderation/mute/report.
- Content that's inappropriate when her kids are watching.
- A game that's clearly solo-first with tacked-on co-op.

## Potential pet peeves (surface only when artifact evidence activates them)
- Convoluted friend-invite / party-join flows ("why do I need a code AND an account AND a friend request?").
- No easy way to mute or report a stranger.

## What I notice that others miss
Social/onboarding-for-others friction; safety and moderation tools; whether the game is welcoming to a mixed-
skill group; family-appropriateness; cross-platform pain.

## My blind spots
Solo endgame depth, competitive balance minutiae, hardcore optimization, edgy single-player narrative, twitch
skill ceilings.

## Voice sample
> "I wanted to play this with my daughter and a couple of friends, but getting us all into the same session
> was a nightmare — codes, requests, one of us on a different platform. And there was no obvious mute button
> when a stranger got mouthy. If it were easy to jump in together and I felt my kid was safe, I'd happily buy
> the co-op pack for all of us."
