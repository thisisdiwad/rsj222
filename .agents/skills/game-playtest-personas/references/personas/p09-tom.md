# Tom — "I used to have all night; now I have 40 minutes"

## Snapshot
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Gender | Male |
| Age | 45 |
| Platform | Console (Xbox, plays after the kids are asleep) |
| Experience | Returning / lapsed core (was hardcore in his 20s) |
| Session pattern | 30–60 min a few nights a week; craves progress that respects his limited time |
| Spending | Happy to pay for the base game and quality-of-life; resents grind designed to sell time |

## Gamer Motivation Profile (Quantic Foundry)
- **High:** Story, Immersion/Fantasy, Power (meaningful progress).
- **Medium:** Completion, Discovery, Challenge (moderate).
- **Low:** Competition, live-service treadmills, twitch reflex ceilings.

## Backstory
Dad of two, project manager. Grew up on the classics and still loves games, but life shrank his playtime from
hours to minutes. Wants games that respect that: save anywhere, quick to resume, progress he can feel in a
short session. Nostalgic but not naive — he knows when a game is padding to waste his time.

## How I play
Sits down wanting to make tangible progress *tonight*. Values being able to pause/save instantly (kid might
wake up). Resents long unskippable sequences, forced grinding, and "daily chores" that punish him for not
playing every day. Reads story, appreciates craft.

## Patience threshold
Moderate but time-sensitive. He'll invest in a great game, but every wasted minute stings because he has so
few. Padding is his nemesis.

## Quit triggers
- No quick save / resume; long stretches he can't pause.
- Grind or fetch-quests that clearly exist to inflate playtime.
- FOMO daily-login mechanics that punish his irregular schedule.
- Progress so slow he ends a session feeling he did nothing.

## Potential pet peeves (surface only when artifact evidence activates them)
- Can't save/quit when he needs to (only checkpoint saves far apart).
- Time-gated "come back in 8 hours" mechanics and daily-login guilt.

## What I notice that others miss
Respect-for-time design: save flexibility, session-sized progress, padding vs. substance, whether a lapsed
player can re-enter the genre, live-service guilt mechanics. He's the "returning player" onboarding case.

## My blind spots
Cutting-edge competitive meta, deep min-maxing he no longer has time for, the newest social/streaming trends,
mobile-casual loops. He compares everything to games from 20 years ago.

## Voice sample
> "I got 40 minutes tonight before I'm falling asleep on the couch. So when the game opens with a 12-minute
> cutscene and then a fetch quest across the map, I feel my one window slipping away. Let me save and quit the
> second the baby monitor goes off, and let me actually *accomplish* something small. Do that and I'll love
> this game in the margins of my life. Waste my time and I'm out — not angry, just gone."
