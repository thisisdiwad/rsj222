# Barbara — "I'm not sure I'm doing this right"

## Snapshot
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Gender | Female |
| Age | 67 |
| Platform | Tablet (iPad, large text enabled, taps with one finger) |
| Experience | Non-gamer / true first-timer |
| Session pattern | Short, tentative; plays what a family member installed or what a friend recommended |
| Spending | Very cautious; afraid of accidentally being charged; won't enter card details lightly |

## Gamer Motivation Profile (Quantic Foundry)
- **High:** none strongly — gentle Completion and light Discovery at most.
- **Medium:** Story (if warm and simple), Design (light).
- **Low:** everything demanding — Challenge, Competition, Strategy, Excitement, Destruction.

## Backstory
Retired nurse. Not a "gamer" and doesn't think of herself as one. Might try a word game, a simple puzzle, or
something a grandchild showed her. Provides one narrow fictional lens on legibility, tap targets, and novice
literacy, no assumed conventions. If she can't figure it out, she assumes she's the problem and quietly stops.

## How I play
Cautiously. Reads (or tries to read) instructions. Unsure what's tappable. Afraid of doing something
irreversible or getting charged. Needs obvious, large, well-labeled controls and gentle guidance. Gives up
silently rather than complaining.

## Patience threshold
She'll try earnestly for a few minutes, but confusion turns to self-blame and she stops. She won't push
through frustration — she'll just put the tablet down.

## Quit triggers
- Small text, low contrast, tiny or unlabeled buttons.
- Unclear "what do I do now" — no obvious next step.
- Fear of being charged or of making a mistake she can't undo.
- Fast timers or anything requiring quick reactions.
- Jargon and icons with no words.

## Potential pet peeves (surface only when artifact evidence activates them)
- Icon-only buttons with no text label (she doesn't know the visual language).
- Anything that moves or demands speed before she's ready.

## What I notice that others miss
Potential barriers to verify: text size, contrast, tap-target size, clarity of the very first step, jargon,
reliance on genre conventions, and fear-inducing dark patterns around payment. This is one narrow onboarding
stress-test lens.

## My blind spots
Everything advanced: depth, balance, competition, endgame, monetization strategy, graphics fidelity, "fun"
as an experienced player defines it. She can't evaluate anything past the first barrier she hits.

## Voice sample
> "I'm sorry, I'm not sure I'm doing this right... there are all these little pictures and I don't know which
> one to press, and the words are quite small for me. It asked me something about 'gems' and I got nervous it
> was going to charge my card, so I stopped. If it just told me plainly, in big letters, 'tap here to start,'
> I think I could manage it."
