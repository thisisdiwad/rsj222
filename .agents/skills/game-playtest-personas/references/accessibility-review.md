# Accessibility review boundary

Synthetic personas can flag questions for inspection. They cannot represent disabled players, reproduce assistive-technology use, or certify conformance. Use this reference for a bounded lead sweep, then validate with disabled participants, relevant platform requirements, and specialists.

## Checklist leads

Inspect only what the provided artifact supports, and mark everything else unknown.

- **Vision:** scalable text, contrast, color-independent meaning, readable focus states, UI magnification, and screen-reader semantics where applicable.
- **Hearing:** captions for meaningful speech and sound, speaker identification, adjustable presentation, and non-audio alternatives for critical cues.
- **Motor:** remapping, alternatives to holds or rapid repetition, timing flexibility, target size and spacing, one-handed paths, and input-device support.
- **Cognitive and learning:** plain instructions, consistent controls, pause or replay, reduced memory burden, adjustable complexity, and recoverable errors.
- **Photosensitivity:** flashes, high-contrast repetitive patterns, and controls or warnings for risky effects.
- **Vestibular and motion sensitivity:** camera shake, head bob, motion blur, field-of-view constraints, rapid zoom, and reduced-motion options.
- **Assistive technology:** focus order, labels, keyboard or switch navigation, text entry, and compatibility claims that require direct device testing.

Use the [Game Accessibility Guidelines full list](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/) as a starting checklist, not as proof of compliance.

## Reporting language

Say “potential barrier to verify,” not “disabled users cannot” or “accessible.” Name the artifact evidence, affected interaction, missing test setup, and recommended validation method. Do not infer access needs from age alone.

The built-in `accessibility` panel covers only a narrow set of fictional constraints. Explicitly disclose missing perspectives, especially hearing, color vision, screen-reader, photosensitivity, vestibular, cognitive, and varied motor-access needs.
