# Domain pack — economics, psychology & social sciences

## Sources & databases
- **OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science**; **NBER** & **SSRN**
  (working papers — preprints), **RePEc/EconPapers**, **PsycINFO**, **ERIC**
  (education), **Campbell Collaboration** (systematic reviews of social
  interventions), government/OECD/World Bank statistics.

## Field specifics
- Weight **causal identification**: RCTs/field experiments > natural experiments
  / diff-in-diff / IV / RDD > plain regression/correlational. State the
  identification strategy and its assumptions.
- Prefer **pre-registered** studies and **Registered Reports**; check for
  **replication** (the field has a known replication crisis).
- Report **effect sizes and confidence intervals**, not just significance;
  consider external validity (does it generalise beyond the sample/country/era?).
- **WEIRD-sample caveat**: many findings come from Western, Educated,
  Industrialised, Rich, Democratic samples — flag limited generalisability.

## Red flags specific to the field
- **p-hacking, HARKing, forking paths, publication bias**, underpowered studies,
  small-N lab effects that failed to replicate (see
  `references/statistics-red-flags.md`).
- Working papers cited as settled (NBER/SSRN are not peer-reviewed); advocacy
  think-tank reports with a policy agenda (treat as `OBJECT-OF-STUDY`).
- Over-generalising a single-country/context result to universal claims.

## Consensus & framing
Consensus is often **contested or context-dependent**; report the range of
credible estimates and the conditions under which each holds, rather than a
single point answer. Lean on meta-analyses and Campbell/Cochrane-style reviews
where they exist.
