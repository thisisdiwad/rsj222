# Domain pack — climate, environment & energy

## Sources & databases
- **IPCC Assessment Reports** (AR6 and successors) and Special Reports — the
  primary consensus anchor; cite the specific chapter/SPM statement and its
  calibrated-uncertainty language.
- Peer-reviewed literature via **OpenAlex / Web of Science / Scopus**, journals
  such as *Nature Climate Change*, *Nature Geoscience*, *Science*, *GRL*, *ACP*,
  *ERL*. Data from **NASA GISS, NOAA, Copernicus/ECMWF, Berkeley Earth, WMO**.
- National academies and academy joint statements; **IPBES** for biodiversity.

## Field specifics
- Use the IPCC **calibrated uncertainty language** verbatim when reporting:
  likelihood (*virtually certain ≥99%*, *very likely ≥90%*, *likely ≥66%* …) and
  confidence (low/medium/high/very high). Map your GRADE-style rating onto it.
- Distinguish **observations** from **model projections**, and projections
  across **scenarios** (SSP/RCP) — never quote a projected number without its
  scenario and time horizon.
- Separate **attribution** (has it happened / is it human-caused) from
  **projection** (what may happen) from **impact/adaptation** questions.

## Red flags specific to the field
- **Manufactured doubt**: cherry-picked short time windows, single stations,
  confusing weather with climate, recycled long-debunked talking points,
  industry- or think-tank-funded contrarian "reports" (treat as
  `OBJECT-OF-STUDY`, never evidence).
- Non-peer-reviewed blog science (both alarmist and denialist); preprints not yet
  assessed.
- Over-precise single-model results presented without ensemble spread.

## Consensus anchor
There is overwhelming scientific consensus that recent warming is real and
primarily human-caused; represent this as settled, and confine genuine debate to
where it actually exists (regional projections, climate sensitivity ranges,
tipping-point timing, specific impacts).
