# Excluding sponsored, predatory, and non-scholarly sources

The goal of this repository is evidence, not persuasion. Screen out anything
whose purpose is to sell, rank, or promote rather than to report findings.

## Never use as evidence of fact

- **Sponsored / "partner" / "paid" content** and native advertising.
- **Press releases and PR wire** (PR Newswire, Business Wire, EurekAlert item
  reproduced uncritically) — trace to the underlying paper instead.
- **Vendor blogs, product pages, whitepapers, case studies** — marketing.
- **SEO listicles / "best of" affiliate content** ("Top 10 …", comparison sites
  monetised by referrals).
- **Op-eds, editorials, opinion columns, personal blogs, social media.**
- **AI-generated content farms** and aggregators that don't link primary sources.

These may be *quoted as objects of study* ("Company X claims Y"), but never
counted as evidence that the claim is true. Always mark them as such.

## Predatory / low-quality journal red flags

A source being in a "journal" is not enough. Watch for:

- Not indexed in **DOAJ**, **Scopus**, **Web of Science**, **PubMed/MEDLINE**,
  or the relevant field database.
- Publisher on cautionary lists / not a member of **COPE**, **OASPA**, or
  **WAME**; missing from **Think-Check-Submit** style checks.
- Fake or inflated metrics ("index copernicus value", invented impact factors).
- Extremely fast, non-substantive peer review; spammy solicitation; hidden or
  exorbitant APCs; fake editorial boards; misleading journal names mimicking
  reputable titles.
- No DOI, or DOIs that don't resolve via Crossref.

When unsure, prefer a source that is (a) indexed in a major database and (b)
has a resolvable DOI. If neither holds, treat the claim as unverified.

## Conflict-of-interest and funding checks

- Read the **funding** and **competing interests** statements. Industry-funded
  studies are not automatically invalid, but weight them accordingly and look
  for independent replication.
- Be alert to **manufactured doubt** — coordinated campaigns that manufacture
  the appearance of scientific controversy (tobacco, climate, etc.). A handful
  of contrarian, industry-linked papers do not overturn a consensus built on
  the wider literature.

## Quick disposition

For each candidate source, record one of:
`EVIDENCE` (scholarly, usable) · `BACKGROUND` (context only) ·
`OBJECT-OF-STUDY` (a claim being examined, not evidence) · `REJECT` (with reason).
