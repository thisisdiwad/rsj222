# Statistical & methodological red flags

Quick screen for whether a quantitative result is trustworthy. Presence of a flag
doesn't refute a study, but it lowers the weight and demands corroboration.

## Design & power
- **Underpowered** (small N, no power calculation) — "positive" small studies
  overestimate effects (winner's curse).
- **No control / inappropriate comparator**; confounding not addressed.
- **Surrogate outcomes** standing in for what matters.

## Analysis & inference
- **p-hacking / forking paths** — many analyses, only "significant" ones shown.
- **Multiple comparisons** without correction; **subgroup** fishing.
- **HARKing** — hypothesising after results are known; no pre-registration.
- Reporting **p-values without effect sizes or confidence intervals**.
- **Correlation presented as causation**; reverse causation / mediation ignored.
- **Optional stopping**, outcome switching vs. the registered protocol.
- Over-precise point estimates with no uncertainty; ignoring ensemble/CI spread.

## Evidence base
- **Publication bias** — funnel-plot asymmetry, missing null results; check
  trial registries for unpublished trials.
- **Failure to replicate** — a striking single result not independently
  reproduced is provisional.
- **Data dredging on observational data**; small effect sizes with huge samples
  (statistically significant, practically trivial).

## Provenance
- Undisclosed **conflicts of interest / funding**; industry-only evidence.
- **Predatory venue**, no peer review, **retracted/corrected** (check per
  `references/reproducible-search.md` §3).

## What to do
Weight down flagged results; seek higher-tier or replicated evidence; and state
the limitation explicitly in the write-up rather than burying it.
