# scientific-consensus — what the field actually holds

Report the weight of evidence, not a coin-flip between studies. Use the
hierarchy in `references/evidence-hierarchy.md`.

## Method

1. **Anchor on the top of the hierarchy.** Start from systematic reviews,
   meta-analyses, and statements by authoritative bodies (WHO, IPCC, national
   academies, Cochrane, major societies). These aggregate consensus; give them
   primacy over individual studies.
2. **Weigh the body of evidence**, not single papers: consistency across
   independent studies, replication, effect sizes, sample sizes, and study
   quality. One striking result is provisional until reproduced.
3. **Classify the state of the question:**
   - **Settled / strong consensus** — consistent high-quality evidence; expert
     bodies agree.
   - **Emerging consensus** — direction is clear but evidence still maturing.
   - **Genuinely contested** — credible, high-quality evidence on more than one
     side; describe each position and what would resolve it.
   - **Fringe / minority** — a position held by few, weakly supported, or
     contradicted by the wider literature. Report it as such.
4. **State certainty with calibrated language** (GRADE-style: high / moderate /
   low / very low), and give the reason for the rating.

## Guard against distortion

- **No false balance.** Do not present a well-supported consensus and a fringe
  claim as equally weighted. Manufactured doubt (industry-linked, non-replicated
  contrarian papers) does not equal scientific controversy.
- **No false certainty.** Where the science is genuinely open, say so and
  present the competing evidence fairly.
- **Note dissent honestly**, including credible minority views under active
  investigation, with their evidential standing.
- **Time-stamp it.** Consensus can shift; note the date and any live reviews or
  recent results that may move it.

## Output

A clear verdict: *what the consensus is (or that there isn't one)*, the
**strength of evidence**, the main supporting sources (verified), notable
dissent and its standing, and open questions.
