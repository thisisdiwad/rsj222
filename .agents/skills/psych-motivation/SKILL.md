# Motivation & Engagement

Validates that game design sustains intrinsic motivation and
maintains player engagement through evidence-based frameworks.

## Frameworks

### Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)
Three innate needs that drive motivation:
- **Autonomy** — Player has meaningful choices (not just one path)
- **Competence** — Challenges match skill level, success is achievable
- **Relatedness** — Connection to characters, story, or other players

### Flow Theory (Csikszentmihalyi)
Optimal engagement when challenge matches skill:
- Too easy → boredom
- Too hard → frustration
- Just right → flow state
- Provide clear goals and immediate feedback

### Expectancy-Value Theory
Player must both:
- **Expect success** — Believe they CAN do it (not too hard)
- **Value the outcome** — Care about the reward/result

### Attribution Theory (Weiner)
How players explain their success/failure:
- Success should feel earned through EFFORT (not luck)
- Failure should feel fixable (try a different approach)
- Avoid messages that imply innate ability ("you're so smart")
- Prefer effort messages ("great thinking!" "nice try!")

### Operant Conditioning (Skinner)
Ethical reinforcement design:
- **Variable ratio** rewards (unpredictable timing → sustained engagement)
- Ethical bounds: no exploitative mechanics
- Positive reinforcement preferred over punishment
- Celebrate effort, not just correctness

## Validation Checklist

- [ ] Player has meaningful choices (autonomy)
- [ ] Challenges match skill level (flow — not too easy, not too hard)
- [ ] Feedback celebrates effort, not just correctness
- [ ] Rewards are intrinsically meaningful, not manipulative
- [ ] Success feels earned through effort (attribution)
- [ ] Player expects they can succeed (expectancy)
- [ ] Clear goals and immediate feedback (flow)
- [ ] No exploitative reward mechanics
